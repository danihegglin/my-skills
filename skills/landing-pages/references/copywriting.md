# Copywriting

Write like a senior copywriter who has talked to fifty customers: specific, plain, a little opinionated, uneven in rhythm, built from facts rather than formulas.

## Research before writing

The best lines are usually found, not written. Before drafting, collect:

- **Customer words**: reviews (yours and competitors', especially 3-star ones), support tickets, sales call notes, Reddit/forum threads, interview transcripts. Copy exact phrases into a scratch list. Note the words they use for the problem, the old way, and the moment they decided to look for something new.
- **The before/after**: what does their week look like before and after the product? Times, counts, money, stress.
- **The real alternative**: what do they do today if they don't buy? That's what you're competing with.
- **Proof inventory**: every number, quote, logo, rating and award you're allowed to use.

If you have none of this, ask. If the user can't provide it, write from the best available facts and mark guesses `[ASSUMED: ...]` so they can be checked.

## Principles

- **Clear beats clever.** If a reader has to decode the line, it failed. Wordplay is fine only after the meaning is obvious.
- **Specific beats general.** Numbers, names, timeframes, concrete nouns. "Ship invoices the day the job ends" not "Streamline your invoicing".
- **Outcomes beat features**, but name the feature that makes the outcome true. Benefits without mechanism read as hype.
- **Their words beat yours.** If customers say "chasing payments", don't write "accounts receivable optimisation".
- **One idea per section.** If a section needs "and" in its heading, it's probably two sections.
- **Write to one person.** "You", present tense, active voice.
- **Plain words.** Use, help, start, fix, buy, not utilise, facilitate, commence, remediate, purchase.
- **Short, but not choppy.** Mix sentence lengths. Most under 20 words. Grade 6-8 reading level even for technical audiences: experts skim too.
- **Honest.** No invented stats, testimonials, user counts, awards, or "trusted by" logos. Missing proof is `[NEED: ...]`.

## Headlines

The headline does one thing: make the right reader think "that's for me, and I want it".

**Tests every hero headline must pass:**

1. **Five-second test**: someone in the target audience can say what the product does after reading only the headline and subhead.
2. **Swap test**: would it work unchanged on a competitor's site? Then it's not saying anything. Rewrite around the difference.
3. **"Now you can" test**: "Now you can {headline}" should read as a real, desirable new ability.
4. **Say it out loud**: would a founder say this sentence to a customer across a table?

**Patterns that work** (use as starting points, not templates):

- **Outcome + without pain**: "Payroll for 10-person teams, done in 4 minutes"
- **Category + audience**: "The CRM for agencies that bill by the hour"
- **The new ability**: "Every customer call, searchable"
- **Name the pain**: "Still reconciling Stripe payouts by hand?"
- **Specific claim**: "Close your books in 2 days, not 9"

**Rules:**

- 4-10 words is the sweet spot. Long is fine if every word earns its place.
- Concrete verbs and nouns; no stacked adjectives.
- No em dashes, no colons that set up a reveal, no exclamation marks.
- The product name usually doesn't belong in the headline. The logo is right there.
- Big claims need proof within one scroll. "The fastest X" needs a benchmark next to it.

Write at least 10 headline options, then pick. The first three are always the obvious ones.

## Subheads

The subhead answers "how?" and "for whom?" that the headline left open. 1-2 sentences, 15-30 words.

> **Headline**: Sell more. For longer.
> **Subhead**: Gift checkout, card rescue and a pause button for craft beer clubs, beauty boxes and every subscription that ships.

## Body copy and sections

- **Section headings are claims**, not labels. "Features" → "Set it up once, then forget it exists". "Testimonials" → "Shops that switched kept 23% more subscribers".
- **Eyebrows** (small labels above headings) are optional navigation aids, not a place for a second slogan. Keep them to 1-3 words or drop them.
- **Feature blocks**: benefit as heading, one or two sentences of mechanism, one proof point or detail. 25-50 words total.
- **Steps**: verb first. "Connect Stripe", "Pick your offers", "Go live".
- **Lists**: parallel grammar, front-load the important word.
- **Numbers**: use numerals (7, not seven), include units, round only when precision doesn't matter ("~2 min" is fine; "40% faster" needs a source).
- **Objection handling**: say the objection in the reader's voice, then answer it plainly. "Switching sounds painful. We import your subscribers from Recharge in one click."

## CTAs and microcopy

- Button: verb + what they get. "Start free trial", "Get the checklist", "Book a demo".
- Under the button: risk reducer. "14 days free. No card." / "Cancel anytime." / "Setup takes 10 minutes."
- Form labels and errors are copy too. "Enter a work email so we can send your invite" beats "Invalid input".
- Confirmation states say what happens next: "Check your inbox. The link expires in 24 hours."
- Empty, loading and error states in demos deserve real copy.

## Voice

Decide two things before writing, and write them in the design memo:

- **Formality**: casual / friendly-professional / formal
- **Personality**: two or three traits with their opposites, e.g. "confident, not loud; warm, not cute; technical, not jargon-heavy"

Then keep it consistent. Headlines can be bolder than body. Legal and error copy can be plainer. Humour only if it fits the brand and costs no clarity.

## AI tells: never write these

Readers spot generated copy instantly and then distrust everything near it.

**Structures**

1. **Contrast reveals**: "It's not X, it's Y." "Not just X, but Y." "X isn't about Y. It's about Z." State the positive claim directly.
2. **Self-answered questions and colon reveals**: "The result? 3× faster." "The best part: it learns." Just say it.
3. **Negation lists**: "No setup. No contracts. No headaches." Say what *does* happen. One real absence near the CTA ("No card required") is fine.
4. **Trailing pile-ons**: "...from the data you already have, no exports, no spreadsheets, no second copy." End the sentence at the claim.
5. **Rule-of-three everywhere**: "Faster, smarter, simpler." One triad per page, max, and only when there are really three things.
6. **Fragment cascades**: "Fast. Reliable. Yours." One fragment per section, max.
7. **"With X, you can Y"** as the opener for every feature.
8. **"Whether you're a X or a Y..."**, **"In today's fast-paced world..."**, **"Imagine a world where..."**, **"Meet X, the..."**, **"Introducing..."**, **"Here's the thing"**, **"Say goodbye to..."**, **"X, reimagined"**, **"From X to Y, we've got you covered"**.

**Words** (replace each with the fact it stands in for):

seamless, effortless, robust, powerful, cutting-edge, next-generation, revolutionary, game-changing, innovative, unlock, unleash, supercharge, elevate, empower, leverage, streamline, optimize, transform, harness, delve, journey, landscape, realm, tapestry, holistic, synergy, best-in-class, world-class, all-in-one, one-stop, take it to the next level, at scale, in seconds (unless measured).

**Formatting**

- Em dashes in headlines, subheads, buttons or short UI copy. (Sparingly in long body text is fine.)
- Emoji as bullet points or in headings.
- Sparkle ✨ icons or "AI-powered" as a headline claim. Say what the AI actually does.
- Every heading in Title Case. Use sentence case.
- Exclamation marks.

## Editing pass

Before handing over copy:

1. Search the draft for every pattern in the list above.
2. Read every sentence over 20 words; split any that does two jobs.
3. Delete the first sentence of each paragraph and see if it's better. It often is.
4. Replace every adjective you can with a number or a fact.
5. Run the swap test on every section heading.
6. Read it aloud. Anything you stumble on, rewrite.

Fix problems from facts. Swapping a banned word for a synonym just creates a new tell.

## What to deliver

- **Page copy** in reading order, by section, ready to drop into the content file
- **Page title** (≤ 60 chars) and **meta description** (≤ 155 chars), plus OG title/description if different
- **2-3 alternatives** for the headline and primary CTA, each with a one-line rationale
- **Short annotations** on the non-obvious choices
- **List of `[NEED: ...]` and `[ASSUMED: ...]` markers**
